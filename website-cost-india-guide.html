How Much Does a Business Website Cost in India?

A 2026 pricing guide — what drives the cost, typical ranges, and what to ask before you hire.

Venova Digitals·August 18, 2026

"How much does a website cost?" is one of the first questions almost every business owner asks us — and it's also one of the hardest to answer honestly with a single number. A one-page site for a local service business and a full e-commerce platform with a product catalogue and payment integration are both "websites," but they can differ in cost by a factor of ten or more. This guide breaks down what actually drives the price, the rough bands you should expect to see in India in 2026, and the questions worth asking before you sign with any agency — including us.

What Actually Determines the Price

Before looking at numbers, it helps to understand what you're really paying for. A website's cost is driven by a handful of factors, and most price differences between quotes come down to one or more of these:

  • Custom design vs. template. A site built from a pre-made template and customised with your colours and content will always cost less than one designed from scratch around your brand and content.
  • Functionality. A static brochure site is simplest. Add a booking system, a product catalogue, user accounts, or payment processing, and the build gets meaningfully more complex — and more expensive.
  • Content management. Do you need to be able to edit pages, add blog posts, or update pricing yourself? A site with an admin dashboard costs more to build than one where every text change requires a developer.
  • Volume of content. Ten pages of original copy and photography take longer to design and populate than a three-page site.
  • Performance and SEO work. A site that's genuinely optimised — fast load times, proper meta tags, schema markup, mobile-first design — takes more careful engineering than one that just "looks fine" on a laptop screen.

Typical Price Bands in India (2026)

These are broad, agency-observed ranges rather than fixed rates — actual quotes will vary by agency, region, and scope. Use them as a sanity check when you're comparing proposals, not as a quote.

Landing page / one-pager

A single scrolling page — hero, services, contact — is the fastest and cheapest option, typically suited to a new business or a specific campaign. This is the right choice when you need something live quickly and don't yet need multiple pages or a content management system.

Small business brochure site

Multiple pages (home, about, services, contact), custom design matched to your brand, and basic on-page SEO. This is the most common request we get, and the price here depends heavily on how many pages and how much original content and photography you're providing versus asking the agency to source.

Business site with admin dashboard / CMS

Everything in a brochure site, plus the ability to edit content, add blog posts, or manage a simple product list yourself without contacting a developer every time. The added engineering for a proper admin panel is usually the single biggest jump in price after moving from a landing page to a multi-page site.

E-commerce or booking platform

Product catalogues, cart and checkout, payment gateway integration, order management, and often a customer-facing account system. This is the most involved build category, and pricing depends heavily on whether you need a custom-built platform or can use an existing e-commerce framework as a base.

Costs People Forget to Ask About

The build cost is rarely the whole story. Before signing, make sure you know the answer to each of these:

  • Domain and hosting renewal. These are usually annual costs, separate from the one-time build fee. Ask what they'll be after year one.
  • Payment gateway fees. If you're taking payments online, the gateway provider (not your web agency) will typically charge a small percentage per transaction.
  • Maintenance and updates. Who fixes something if it breaks six months after launch? Is that included, or billed separately?
  • Content writing and photography. If you don't have professional photos or finished copy ready, factor in the cost — or time — of producing them.
  • Who owns the account. Make sure the domain registration and hosting account are in your name, not the agency's. This matters far more than people realise if you ever want to switch providers.

Questions to Ask Before You Hire

  1. Will I own the domain, hosting account, and source code outright?
  2. What exactly does "SEO-optimised" mean in this quote — is it just meta tags, or does it include page speed, schema markup, and mobile performance?
  3. How many rounds of revisions are included before extra charges apply?
  4. What happens after launch — is there a support period, and what does it cover?
  5. Can I see examples of sites you've built that are still live and performing well today?

When Cheaper Is Fine — and When It Isn't

If you need a simple, credible online presence fast — a landing page to validate an idea, a temporary campaign page, a placeholder while you plan something bigger — a lower-cost, template-based option is a reasonable choice, not a compromise. Where it stops making sense is when the site is meant to be your primary sales channel, when you need it to rank in search over time, or when you'll be relying on it to process real transactions. In those cases, the gap between a cheap build and a properly engineered one shows up later — in load times, in search rankings, and in how much you end up paying a second time to fix it.
